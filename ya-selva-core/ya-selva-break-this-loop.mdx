## The relationship

> Ya Selva is not Break This Loop's host, owner, or aesthetic.
> He is its **first and most visible participant** — the thesis performed by one person.

| BTL claims | Ya Selva demonstrates |
|---|---|
| anyone, anywhere, can add their piece | a guy who shows up anywhere and makes something |
| strangers become collaborators | ten people gather under a bridge and sing |
| found sound, any culture, any medium | street lamps as percussion, field samples, live loops |
| it travels | he wanders |

**Every Ya Selva video demonstrates the premise without being a BTL product.**
A participant is not a brand. A participant is proof.

Which is why he doesn't carry BTL's aesthetic:

> **Ya Selva is a person. Break This Loop is a place.**
> An artist has a sound. A platform hosts many.
> Burning Man does not sound like its founders.

## The loop

```
YA SELVA collects      →  the archive
YA SELVA sings         →  his songs, made from what he found
BREAK THIS LOOP        →  what happens when everyone else
                          gets to do the same thing with the same material
```

**Graceland is the template.** Paul Simon traveled, recorded local musicians in
their own idiom, and then made *his own songs* out of it. Not a field-recording
album. Not a solo album. Both, undiluted.

The bard doesn't collect *instead* of singing.
He collects, and the songs are what the journey produces.

> ⚠ Graceland is also the most-argued-about case of the extraction question.
> **Credit, consent, compensation from day one** — that's what separates the
> record people love from the one people litigate.

## Two kinds of capture, one night

This is the part that's easy to lose. BTL needs **isolated** contributions —
one person, clean audio, clean video, on the grid — to work as remixable stems.
A room singing together is beautiful, but it isn't a Lego.

```
THE CIRCLE + THE SHOW   →  community footage  →  proof, marketing, mystique
THE BREAKOUT ROOMS      →  isolated stems     →  the remixable archive
```

**That's why the breakout rooms exist as their own segment.**
They're the isolation booth, disguised as an experience — one person at a time,
per room, to click, while the rest of the night happens around them.

Both come out of one night. Neither substitutes for the other.

## How he explains it on stage

Not as a pitch. As what he's already doing.

> *"Everything you're hearing tonight has pieces of other cities in it.
> There's a drummer in Lebanon in this song. There's a dancer in Manila.
> Later tonight, some of you are going to be in the next one."*

The archive is audible in the set, so the invitation is self-evident.

---

## The rules

1. **The night is the atom.** Everything else is downstream of one night existing.
2. **Never cut the performance.** Without the set it's a filming session people attend as a favor.
3. **Capture and turnaround are somebody else's job.** If he runs the room *and* manages the shoot, the model already failed.
4. **Use the warm start. Never let one scene be the only channel.**
5. **Seeding is legitimate. Pretending it's arriving live isn't.**
6. **Credit, consent, compensation from day one.**
7. **Historic accumulates. It doesn't arrive.** Whitacre opened with 185 singers from 12 countries.
