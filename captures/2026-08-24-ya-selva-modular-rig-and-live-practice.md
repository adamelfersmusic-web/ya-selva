## Immediate synthesis

The strongest insight in the capture is that the rig should be **modular by scale**, but the root must stay extremely small.

### Root practice

> **Adam + journal + guitar + phone.**

That is the true minimum viable Ya Selva rig.

It is enough to:
- leave the house
- notice something
- write
- play
- capture an idea
- meet someone
- make contact with the world

### Portable creation rig

Add:
- laptop
- 2-in / 2-out interface
- one vocal / lapel mic
- headphones if needed
- tiny controller / pads if useful
- optional tiny speaker

At that point Ya Selva can write, arrange, loop, record guitar and voice, make beats, capture field material, and build a real performance almost anywhere.

### Street / town-square expansion

Add selectively:
- Bose S1 or other portable speaker
- power bank
- shaker / compact percussion
- cajon if traveling by car
- perhaps a very small guitar effects chain

Now the same practice can become a street performance, town-square jam, or spontaneous public set without changing its underlying architecture.

### Festival / serious live expansion

Ableton becomes the brain.

The same core can scale into:
- backing tracks
- live looping
- guitar
- voice
- percussion
- field recordings
- guest musicians
- local collaborators
- multiple inputs
- bigger amplification

The important design principle is that the festival rig is not a separate identity. It is simply the small rig with modules added.

> **The smallest version should naturally grow toward the biggest version.**

## Ableton as the live brain

There is a clear distinction between production software preference and the needs of the living project.

Logic may remain useful for writing, detailed production, and familiar studio work.

But Ya Selva likely needs a live architecture that can travel from:

> woods → street → room → ecstatic dance → festival → opening slot

Ableton is compelling because it can hold looping, stems, backing material, routing, live control, field recordings, instruments, and guest inputs inside one scalable performance system.

Possible long-term workflow:

> write wherever inspiration happens → produce where useful → move stems/material into Ableton → perform and mutate the work live

No need to force a total software identity decision immediately.

## Guitar as the non-negotiable instrument

Guitar appears to be the essential physical instrument because it is the instrument of deepest fluency and improvisation.

Possible future variations:
- acoustic guitar
- electric guitar
- hybrid acoustic/electric guitar
- octave guitar for bass-like roles
- e-bow / bowed guitar textures
- tiny modeling / amp solution
- minimal delay / overdrive chain

But the principle is not to build the pedalboard first.

> **Body + guitar + voice are the irreducible live core.**

## Percussion and human energy

A recurring insight is that programmed rhythm is not equivalent to a human body striking, shaking, or moving something.

Future compact percussion possibilities:
- shaker
- tiny hand percussion
- pad-based hand percussion
- HandSonic-style device
- contact-mic instrument
- custom object that converts physical strikes into MIDI / Ableton triggers

The exact instrument can evolve later. The deeper requirement is:

> **Keep some human movement in the rhythm.**

## Field recordings

Field recording belongs naturally in the practice.

Not as an aesthetic gimmick, but because leaving the room exposes the music to sounds that could never be invented inside the room:

- insects
- crickets
- wind
- rooms
- streets
- machines
- voices
- crowds
- water
- environmental rhythm

This connects directly to the Ya Selva rule:

> **What entered this song that could not have entered it if I had stayed home?**

## The behavioral unlock

The mobile rig may be one of the biggest practical unlocks in the whole project because it turns an abstract artistic identity into a repeatable action.

> **guitar on back**
> **bag on shoulder**
> **door closes**

The first practice is not "build the show."

The first practice is:

> **re-enter the world.**

Momentum should come from a workable unit that naturally builds toward the larger vision, rather than waiting until the larger vision is completely engineered.

## The assignment

The final line of the capture may be the deepest one:

> **Your responsibility as a human is to make the sound, invite other people into it, document it, move, travel, and push it out while you are alive.**

That is close to the practical assignment of Ya Selva.

### Synthesis

The everyday Ya Selva rig should likely be **Move-first, not laptop-first**.

For ordinary re-entry, wandering, field capture, pop-up performance, and low-friction creation, the portable stack can be:

> **Move + tiny front end/mixer + guitar + mic + phone**

Optional additions:
- flute
- tiny percussion
- small speaker
- power bank

The important shift is architectural:

> **Move is not a separate mini-version of the project. It is the first stage of the same musical process.**

Material can begin on the street, in a park, at a friend's house, or while traveling:
- sample the environment
- build a beat
- capture guitar or voice
- sketch the harmonic/melodic idea
- perform it immediately
- record a synced phone performance

Then the same musical material can move upward into Ableton Live and Push when the work asks for more depth.

The expansion path is continuous:

> **Note → Move → Live + Push → ecstatic dance / full live set / festival**

Not separate workflows. Not separate identities. Not "demo mode" versus "real music."

It is one organism gaining resolution and scale.

### Everyday mode

> **Move + guitar + mic + phone**

Fast enough that leaving the house still feels easy.

### Pop-up performance mode

> **Move + tiny mixer/front end + guitar + mic + phone + speaker**

The mixer combines live sources for the phone and/or Move workflow. A phone can capture the performance with finished stereo audio already synced to video. Multiple phone angles or repeated takes can be cut around the same tempo/grid.

### Build-out mode

> **Take the same musical material into Ableton Live + Push.**

Now add:
- deeper arrangement
- more tracks
- stems
- additional percussion
- field recordings
- guests
- longer transitions
- live looping / clip structure
- more sophisticated performance control

### Ecstatic dance / larger-show mode

By the time Push and the full Live rig appear, the project has not changed direction. The small field sketch has simply expanded into the larger embodied version.

> **The everyday street version and the ecstatic-dance version are the same process at different scales.**

This is the key design principle:

> **Start small enough to go. Keep the material portable. Expand only when the music asks for it.**

And:

> **The rig does not become a different rig as the project grows. The same musical material gains more channels, more bodies, more amplification, and more space.**
