What this is

You're not coming
to a show.

Break This Loop is one song being built by people all over the world. Musicians, dancers, poets — across more than twenty countries and six continents — each adding a piece to the same track.

It's been growing quietly for two years. On [DATE], it comes to [CITY].

And the people in this room become part of it.

Everything you hear that night has pieces of other cities in it. A drummer in Lebanon. A dancer in Manila. By the end of the night, some of you are in the next one.

The arc of the night

Two halves

01

The Gathering

Doors at seven. We start in a circle — a theme, real questions, small groups. Nobody performs. You meet the people you're about to make something with. Held by [HOST NAME].

02

The Rooms

Four rooms open. Voices. Percussion. Melodic. Texture. You walk into whichever one is yours and you record your piece into the song — no experience needed, no instrument required. Then you hear yourself in it.

03

The Return

Everyone comes back. We play what the room just made — all of it, together, at once. Most people don't expect how that feels.

04

The Show

Ya Selva plays. Footage from every previous city moving behind it. At some point the beat drops out and the whole room sings — and that take goes into the record, stacked with the rooms that came before you.

05

The Jam

Drums come out. Everybody plays. This is where it usually goes somewhere nobody planned.

→

One week later

Your piece drops. Publicly. Credited by name, next to musicians from six continents.

Who's bringing it

Ya Selva

A musician and gathering facilitator — twenty years of song circles, drum circles and improvised choirs, and the founder of Break This Loop, the global collaboration this night belongs to.

He travels with a bag: a sampler, an acoustic guitar, a mic. He records what he finds and makes songs out of it. He's been doing this one room at a time for two decades, which is the only reason a night like this works.

Hosted with [HOST NAME] — who holds the first half of the night. This is their room, their circle, their people. That's the point.
