# The night — run of show

The workshop is the opening act. The recording happens inside the experience,
never as a filming session.

```
DOORS          arrive early — the opt-in tier, capped ~30
  ↓
THE GATHERING  talking circle, small groups, tonight's theme,
               prompts and questions. People connect first. (~45 min)
  ↓
BREAKOUT ROOMS 3-4 producers, 3-4 spaces. People express on the theme.
               THIS IS THE CAPTURE — isolated stems. (~60-90 min)
  ↓
ROOMS CLOSE    everyone converges. Short closing share.
  ↓
THE SHOW       Ya Selva plays. Not an audience — the people who just
               made something together, being celebrated. Footage from
               previous cities woven through the set.
  ↓
NEXT WEEK      the pieces drop. Contributors paired with collaborators
               elsewhere in the world.
```

## Why the sequence works

A normal show asks strangers to watch, and participation has to be extracted.
Here participation happens before anyone is in audience-mode. By the time he plays,
the room has talked, opened up, made something, and been witnessed.

The celebration lands because the room earned it. And the recording works because
nobody is being filmed — they're having an experience that happens to be captured.

## Ticketing

Two self-selecting tiers: a Village ticket (arrive early, do everything, be in the
piece, capped at room capacity) and a show ticket. No forced participation — that's
what kills these formats.

## Non-negotiable

Producers run the rooms. An editor or agent pipeline handles turnaround.
Adam facilitates and performs. If he is running the room AND managing the shoot,
the model has already failed.
