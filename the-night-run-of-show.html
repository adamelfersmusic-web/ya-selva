The principle

The workshop
earns the concert

A normal show asks strangers to watch, and participation has to be extracted. Here it happens before anyone is in audience mode — by the time the set starts, the room has talked, opened up, made something, and been witnessed.

Which is also why the recording works. Nobody is being filmed. They're having a night that happens to be captured.

The schedule

16:30Load-in

Build the rooms

Click distributed. Drone patch loaded in every room. Same tempo, same key, everywhere.

18:15Brief

Producer briefing

Tempo, key, drone, take length, file naming. Everyone leaves with the same session template. This is the meeting that makes the night repeatable.

18:45Check

Soundcheck

Kit at circle-appropriate volume — contact-mic triggers, not a full acoustic kit.

19:00Doors

Village tier only

~30 people. Tea. No music yet. The list is the ticket.

19:2045 min

The Gathering

Circle. Tonight's theme, prompts, small groups. It gets real. Nobody performs. People connect with each other before they connect with the music.

20:0585 min

Breakout rooms

Three or four producers, three or four spaces. People self-select. Roughly 7–8 per room, 6–10 minutes each. The isolation booth, disguised as an experience.

21:3015 min

Rooms close · rough stack

Producers bounce their room to one stereo file. No editing. Handed to the capture lead.

21:4515 min

Closing circle

Groups of three, timed prompts — what was it like to make something tonight? Then the stack plays once, loud, with nobody talking. Everyone hears themselves inside a thing they made without knowing they were making it.

22:00Doors

Show tier arrives

General admission joins. Room roughly doubles.

22:15The set

Ya Selva plays

Footage from every previous city woven through the visuals. The room isn't an audience — it's the people who just made something together, being celebrated.

~22:503 takes

The hook drop

Beat cuts. Rim click on 2 and 4, drone underneath, click in one ear, one out to hear the room. Thirty-plus people at full volume. Three takes, sixteen bars. Omni rolling the whole time — never stop between takes.

23:15Climax

Community jam

Percussion out. Selected players in the crowd, not on stage. One point in the set, not at the door.

00:00Out

Cards consolidated

Before anyone leaves. One person's only job.

+3 daysPrivate

Cuts back to contributors

Individually. By name. This is when a stranger becomes a collaborator.

+7 daysPublic

The version drops

Same song, this city's players. Contributors tagged. Behind-the-scenes alongside.

Inside a breakout room

The isolation booth

Per person

1. Producer plays the bed at session tempo + key, drone under it   0:30
2. Contributor hears it once through headphones                    0:30
3. Take 1 — 8 or 16 bars                                           0:30
4. Takes 2 and 3 — different feel each time                        2:00
5. PLAY IT BACK TO THEM. They hear themselves in the song.         1:00
6. Name, contact, consent. Reset.                                  1:00
                                                        ≈ 6–10 min

Producer protocol — one page, same in every room

New audio track  →  NAME IT WITH THEIR NAME   ← this is the credit list
Arm it           →  three takes on three tracks
Keep the best    →  mute the others
END              →  edit nothing. Bounce the room to one stereo file.

Track name = person's name. That single habit produces the credit list, the file naming and the tagging list with zero extra work. Nothing gets edited. Thirty people should sound like thirty people.

The move that makes it repeatable

The rooms
are the stems

Eight people layered on the same sixteen bars is mud. Four differentiated groups layer into an arrangement.

Room 01

Voices

Hooks, harmonies, chants, whispers

Room 02

Percussion

Hands, shakers, found objects, body

Room 03

Melodic

Horn, strings, keys, charango

Room 04

Texture

Spoken word, poetry, field sound, movement

One decision solves four problems: density, self-selection ("I want to be in the voice room" is a real choice), the +7 day build, and a playback that assembles in three minutes because the stacking already happened, in parallel, while the night was going on.

The hook drop

Already pulled off in a room of twenty, with one AirPod, conducting by hand and stomping the downbeat. It synced to the grid perfectly and shipped in the actual production.

The stomp is the elegant part: the conductor and the slate are the same motion. It holds the room visually and puts a hard transient in every recording.

Why five cities stack

same click  →  same tempo
same key    →  same drone holding the root
same hook   →  learnable by strangers in sixty seconds

five rooms of thirty people, screaming, on one grid.

Stacked recordings of people alone in bedrooms sound like a lot of people alone. This sounds like a lot of people together, because it is. You cannot fake the sound of a room.

The one thing to protect

Click fixes tempo, not pitch. Keep a quiet drone under the a cappella even though it isn't in the arrangement. Nobody registers it as accompaniment, and it's the only thing holding thirty people to a root across five cities — and you won't be able to hear whether it's working from stage.

Hard-won — treat as law

The instrument
doctrine

Percussion
Hand out freely. Forgiving — a bad player is still in the pocket.
Melodic
Never hand out. One untrained horn or guitar wrecks a room in eight bars.
Voice
The universal one. Nobody can bring it badly. A few bad singers disappear into twenty decent ones.
ControlWhy
Pass out at ONE point in the set — never at the doorLets the climax happen without the jam eating the show
Pre-select the melodic players in each townReal chops only. Anyone can bring a voice.
Selected players stand IN the crowdSound moves from every direction, not one point
Stage in the CENTRE of the roomEverything radiates. Nobody is at the back.

Ya Selva is Break This Loop
at the size of one life

Same mechanics, two scales. One town and a few musicians versus twenty countries and four weeks. Nothing ships to Break This Loop that hasn't worked in a room first.

Which de-risks the whole thing: BTL stops being one enormous bet and becomes an aggregation of things already proven at small scale.

The released version is mostly other people — he might be playing guitar, or just visible facilitating. That's the thesis proving itself instead of announcing itself.

The energy constraint

A full facilitation workshop plus a full concert in one night, and he is the only person who can do both halves. That is the real ceiling on frequency — monthly, not weekly. Which is convenient: Moonlight is a full-moon event. The format and the calendar already agreed.
