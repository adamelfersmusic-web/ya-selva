# Ya Selva — Ableton Ecosystem

## Purpose

This note explains how Ableton Note, Ableton Move, and Ableton Live / Push fit together inside the Ya Selva project.

The point is not to build a complicated studio.
The point is to create a scalable ecosystem that lets the music begin anywhere and grow only when the song asks for it.

────────

## Core principle

> **Start with the smallest thing that keeps the music alive. Scale only when the song asks for it.**

That is the organizing rule.

────────

## 1) Ableton Note — Pocket Sketchbook

Role: the smallest tier in the ecosystem.

What it is:

- phone-based idea capture
- musical sketchbook
- fastest zero-friction entry point
- always with you

Use it for:

- tapping out a beat
- sketching a melody
- capturing a rhythmic idea
- experimenting with sound quickly
- beginning a musical thought when nothing else is with you

Why it matters for Ya Selva:
Note removes the excuse that you do not have your setup.
If the idea arrives, you can start it on the phone.

Short definition:

> **Note = pocket sketchbook**

────────

## 2) Ableton Move — Field Instrument

Role: the walk-out-the-door electronic tier.

What it is:

- standalone
- battery-powered
- portable
- playable
- sample-based
- tactile

Relevant strengths:

- built-in speaker
- built-in mic
- direct sampling
- live input
- 4 tracks
- effects
- presets
- can connect to a speaker
- small enough to live in the bag

Use it for:

- sampling the environment
- building rhythms from found sound
- making field sketches that feel more musical and performable than phone ideas
- composing outside the studio
- minimal live accompaniment
- tiny electronic performance rig

Why it matters for Ya Selva:
Move may be the actual smallest electronic Ya Selva setup.
It is small enough to carry into the world, but deep enough to actually matter.

It keeps the project connected to:

- immediacy
- environment
- rhythm
- movement
- direct encounter

Short definition:

> **Move = field instrument**

────────

## 3) Ableton Live + Push / Controller — Travel Studio + Live Brain

Role: expansion tier.

What it is:

- full production environment
- arrangement and editing space
- live-performance brain
- deep control layer

Use it for:

- expanding strong ideas born in Note or Move
- building full songs and arrangements
- integrating vocals, guitar, flute, percussion, and field recordings
- running full live sets
- scaling from sketch to stage
- controlling more complex performance architecture

Why it matters for Ya Selva:
This is where raw living material becomes a finished work or full show architecture.
But it should not be the mandatory starting point every time.

Short definition:

> **Live + Push = studio / stage brain**

────────

## 4) Acoustic and Human Layer

The Ableton ecosystem is only part of the actual Ya Selva ecosystem.
The deeper root is still:

- voice
- guitar
- flute
- tiny percussion
- frame drums
- found sound
- field recordings
- human participation
- the world itself

The technology exists to support contact with life, not replace it.

────────

## Ecosystem ladder

### Tier 1 — Pocket

Phone + Note

- idea spark
- zero friction
- sketch anywhere

### Tier 2 — Field

Move + instrument + phone

- compose outside
- sample directly
- perform minimally
- connect to a small speaker

### Tier 3 — Travel studio

Move or instrument + laptop + interface + Push / controller

- expand the idea
- build the song
- create a bigger set

### Tier 4 — Full live system

Ableton Live rig + instruments + PA / guests / audience / participation

- street corner
- tunnel
- forest
- small venue
- ecstatic dance
- festival

────────

## The real insight

The ecosystem works because each layer has a distinct job:

- Note catches the spark.
- Move turns the spark into a living field sketch.
- Live / Push expands the field sketch into a song or full performance world.

That means the music can start tiny and scale naturally.

────────

## Ya Selva translation

> **Note = pocket sketchbook**
>
> **Move = field instrument**
>
> **Live / Push = studio + stage brain**

And underneath all of it:

> **The world is still the source material.**

────────

## Governing question

Before adding anything to the system, ask:

> **Does this add major capability without making me less likely to walk out the door?**

If yes, it belongs.
If it increases friction too much, it probably belongs in a higher tier, not the base rig.

────────

## One-line summary

Ableton Note, Move, and Live form a scalable Ya Selva ecosystem: from pocket sketchbook, to field instrument, to full studio/stage brain — so music can begin anywhere and grow only when the song asks for it.
