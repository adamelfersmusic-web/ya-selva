# The Shared DNA — Ya Selva ⟷ Break This Loop

Synthesis. 2026-08-28.

The connective tissue between the two projects. `the-connection.md` says how they
relate structurally (presence-dependent vs presence-independent). This says what they
are made of — the thing underneath both.

## Where this came from

Adam put the Ya Selva poster next to two Break This Loop posters — the Poranguí World
Tour poster, and "Roots in Motion" (a Tokyo-coded artist, red/bone/brutalist, editorial
layout).

> "They're totally different, but for some reason, this was the first time that I could
> kind of start to see it a little bit."

> "they actually have a certain similarity... I actually do kind of think maybe they
> share something in their DNA but I'm not sure I can even quite fully put my finger on
> what it even is honestly."

This doc is the attempt to name it.

## The visual thread — the glyph, not the aesthetic

The two poster worlds are near-opposites:

| | Ya Selva | Roots in Motion (BTL) |
|---|---|---|
| Palette | forest green · gold · orange | red · bone · black |
| Register | botanical, ritual | brutalist, editorial |
| Mood | jungle, ceremonial | urban, streetwear |

One shared element: **the glyph set.** Triangle, circle, wave, dots — in gold, in the
same vertical position on the left edge of both.

This is how a platform brand is supposed to work. BTL is not imposing an aesthetic on
its artists. It carries a **sigil** into whatever world each artist already lives in.
The Tokyo poster doesn't look like Adam's poster and it shouldn't — it looks like *that
artist*, with BTL's mark on it.

**Flag resolved:** "Ancient signals. Modern frequencies." appears on Roots in Motion,
which has nothing to do with Poranguí. So it is genuinely campaign-level BTL language,
not inherited from Adam's nearest reference point.

## The structural rhyme

```
VISUALLY   BTL doesn't impose a look.   It carries a MARK into each artist's own world.
SONICALLY  BTL doesn't impose a sound.  It imposes a METHOD — real human performance,
                                        honored and recombined.
```

Different worlds, same rule. This is why Adam can push Ya Selva as far into his own
aesthetic as he wants and the transition stays fluid rather than strained. **The
platform was never asking for aesthetic conformity.**

## THE SHARED DNA — the actual answer

> **Neither project is about the music. Both are about people making music together.**

World music is the vehicle, not the point.

**Why world music specifically:** those traditions are participatory by design. Drum
circles, call-and-response, clave-based ensemble music — traditions where the line
between performer and audience dissolves *structurally*, as a property of the form, not
as a nice intention layered on top.

Adam's own statement of what he loves: *"I like world music and African music and Latin
beats and flutes and tribal sounding drums."*

**But the attachment isn't timbral.** He didn't fall in love with those traditions
because of how they sound. He fell in love with them because they're the traditions
where everyone plays.

```
BREAK THIS LOOP   that, at planetary scale
YA SELVA          that, in a room
```

Same thing, two scales. Which is the same finding as the played-not-programmed
signature, arrived at from the culture side instead of the production side.

## "The future is ancient"

Adam's own line, offered as a tagline candidate. It states the shared claim exactly:

**Both projects take living traditions and honor them by USING them, not by preserving
them.**

The material isn't a museum piece and it isn't a costume. It's still being made, and
he's making it. That's the whole distinction between this and every adjacent thing in
the same visual neighborhood.

Structurally this is "exploring the intersection of real instruments and modern
production" — with the note that BTL's *recording* layer is often weak by necessity
(contributors recording where they are, on what they have), while the *production*
layer is where the modern half actually lives.

## Why this keeps him out of the crowded lane

The festival/retreat aesthetic is a busy neighborhood, and the generic version of it is
**costume** — borrowed signifiers, nobody actually participating.

Adam has standing most people reaching for this don't: Colombia, Tijuana, Standing Rock,
hundreds of drum circles and song circles facilitated, contributors in 20+ countries.

**The distinction is participation vs. costume.** That isn't a caution to be careful
about — it's the thesis restated. His version is legible because the people are actually
in it.

Consistent with the positioning finding already on file: what actually decides crossover
is **who is standing next to him in the frame** — and the gap flagged there is that
there is not one other human being in any current brand image. That gap is the same gap
as this one. The DNA is participation, and the current visual assets show him alone.

## The channel distinction — keep it deliberate

```
BREAK THIS LOOP   urban-edged · streetwear · global-city coded
YA SELVA          jungle · Bali · conscious festival · yoga retreat · ecstatic dance
```

These are **distribution channels, not identities.** They should stay different on
purpose — the sequencing rule already on file: *the scene can only capture BTL if it is
the ONLY input; use the warm start, never let it be the only channel, make node two
deliberately different.*

The Roots in Motion poster is proof the rule is already working. Node two doesn't look
anything like node one.

## What this settles

1. The projects are not a brand family — they're **one principle at two scales**.
2. Ya Selva is not a side project sharing a color palette with BTL. It's the
   **single-artist proof of BTL's thesis**.
3. "World music" is the wrong name for what Adam is into. **Participatory music** is the
   right one.
4. The BTL tagline concern is closed — it's platform language.
