# The Host Model — how BTL becomes presence-independent

## The move

```
LOCAL HOST   runs THE GATHERING — the whole front half
ADAM         runs the breakout rooms, then plays
```

Don't travel with a facilitator. Partner with the one already in that city.

## Why it's the relationship mechanism, not load-shedding

Booking a venue makes you a visiting act. Handing someone the front half of your night
makes you a collaborator. It says the one thing you can't say any other way:
**I trust you with my room.**

It flips the economics of getting cities. Not "can I have access to your community" but
"here is a stage, a co-billing, and a night better than either of us could run alone."

## The trust is the asset

Walking into a new city as a stranger and asking thirty people to open up, sing in front of
each other, and be recorded is an enormous ask from someone nobody knows. Even with a perfect
format the room stays half-guarded — and **guarded rooms produce guarded takes.**

When the host says "this guy's the real deal, go deep," the room lands there in ten minutes
instead of never. **They are not lending a venue. They are lending credibility.**

It also answers the crossover question: what makes you legible to a room of strangers isn't
the hat or the gear or the résumé. It's that someone they already trust is standing next to you.

## The delegation must be real

GIVE the whole front half — their name on the billing, their format, their prompts, their theme.
NOT a warm-up slot. A host given a token slot vouches weakly. A host who ran the circle vouches
like it's their own thing, because it is.

## The seedling

```
NIGHT 1   they lend credibility, run the Gathering
          → their people are in the release, tagged, by name
NIGHT 2   they know the format. They've seen it work on their own community.
NIGHT 3   they're asking when the next one is. They have opinions about it.
          → they could run the front half without you in the building
```

Nobody recruited them. They learned it by doing it, in their own room, with their own people.

## It kills the permission problem

BTL stalled because every step required someone else to say yes.

```
Each host operates on their own authority, in their own city.
They don't need your permission. You don't need theirs.
THE HOSTS ARE THE YES — and they're saying it to their own people.
```

The gate inverts. What was a bottleneck becomes the distribution.
Whitacre: 12 countries → 58 → 129. Each round leaves behind the people who bring the next one.

## The chain

```
one host → one night → their people in the release
         → a second node → a network
         → BREAK THIS LOOP RUNNING WITHOUT YOU
```

You don't build the ambassador network. You hand the circle to one person in one city
and it starts building itself.
