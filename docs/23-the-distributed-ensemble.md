# Ya Selva — The Distributed Ensemble

## Core idea

> **Ya Selva carries the spine. The ensemble lives in the world.**

Ya Selva is not designed around a fixed touring band that must travel together everywhere.

The portable core travels:
- Adam
- the songs and arrangements
- guitar and voice
- the Ableton performance brain
- the sonic vocabulary
- cues, stems, loops, and participation architecture

The human ensemble is distributed across places.

Over time, Ya Selva builds trusted relationships with master musicians, singers, dancers, facilitators, and other collaborators in different cities. These are not random local guests hired at the last minute. They are recurring collaborators who already know the work, have contributed to it, or understand the project deeply enough to reactivate it when Ya Selva returns.

> **The band becomes a relationship network activated by geography.**

## Same spine, different body

> **Same spine. Different body.**

Dayton should not have exactly the same Ya Selva concert as Bogotá, Lisbon, or Manila.

The stable spine preserves quality:
- core songs
- rehearsed transitions
- core grooves
- sonic identity
- participatory architecture
- visual language
- the portable live system

The local body changes:
- musicians
- voices
- rhythms
- field recordings
- dancers
- stories
- environmental sounds
- improvisations
- audience participation

The city does not decorate the show. It changes the organism.

## The tour becomes composition

> **The tour itself becomes part of the composition.**

A tour stop is not merely another performance of the same set. It becomes another version of the work.

A piece may change across 17 cities, 31 musicians, six years, and repeated returns to the same places. The archive can show how the music evolved through real contact with people and geography.

The accumulation is the proof.

Twenty cities is interesting. Fifty is compelling. Years of returning to the same collaborators turns the concept into history, relationship, and evidence.

> **Unique idea is fragile. Unique body of work is defensible.**

## Portable core + dormant local nodes

The touring architecture is:

> **portable core + dormant local nodes**

Ya Selva arrives with a complete solo organism. The show does not depend on local collaborators in order to function.

Then the local node activates:
- a percussionist joins two songs
- a singer enters one section
- a horn player has a recurring part
- dancers seed movement in the room
- a local ensemble joins the participatory climax
- field recordings from that city enter the set

The collaborators expand the show rather than rescue it.

> **Do not design a portable version of a big show. Design a tiny complete show that the world can expand.**

## Relationship, not sourcing

The model is not:

> find random musicians in every city

It is:

> **build relationships city by city**

A collaborator may first enter through Ya Selva, Break This Loop, a recording session, a workshop, a remix, a field encounter, or a live event.

When Ya Selva returns, the relationship is already there.

The city develops memory.

Early phase:
> assemble musicians locally

Middle phase:
> retain the strongest relationships

Later phase:
> a distributed ensemble exists across cities

Tour:
> activate the relevant node wherever the route goes

## The system as musical spine

The core live system makes this model practical.

Local players can enter a tightly designed performance architecture without requiring a conventional touring-band setup. The central Ableton system can hold the arrangement, loops, scenes, cues, samples, effects, and transitions while live players enter and leave around it.

This preserves authorship and coherence while allowing the body of the show to change.

The system can also support looping, sampling, live mutation, and controlled improvisation around collaborators.

## Break This Loop relationship

The distributed ensemble naturally feeds Break This Loop.

A collaborator can:
- play on a Ya Selva performance
- contribute a part remotely later
- enter a Break This Loop round
- introduce other musicians
- become part of the archive
- connect one city node to another

This creates a relationship flywheel between travel, live performance, recording, participation, and global collaboration.

## Historical precursor

About ten years before this architecture became clear, a Rainbow Gathering elder saw the pattern and advised Adam to assemble the band from the location each time rather than carrying a fixed band everywhere.

The current distributed-ensemble model is a more developed version of that same insight: the stable musical center travels, while the ensemble is assembled from trusted local relationships.

## Governing line

> **Ya Selva carries the spine. The ensemble lives in the world.**
