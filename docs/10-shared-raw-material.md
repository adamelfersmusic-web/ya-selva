# Shared Raw Material — the collection-consent rule

Rule. 2026-08-28.

Operational addendum to `09-the-shared-dna.md`. Small rule, cheap to hold, annoying to
reconstruct later.

## The question Adam raised

> "If I was traveling, there's really nothing saying that I couldn't use some of the
> same sample set in Break This Loop and Ya Selva. There's really not a fucking rule
> about it."

Correct — there isn't one, and he'd be the one writing it. So here it is.

## THE RULE

```
ASK AT THE POINT OF RECORDING, NOT AT THE POINT OF USE.
```

When Adam records someone, he tells them the material may be used in either project —
Ya Selva or Break This Loop — and gets a yes.

Ten seconds. Happens while he's already talking to them. Retires the entire question
permanently: no case-by-case judgment calls later, no going back to someone in Oaxaca
two years on to ask about a track that's already mixed.

## What's settled

```
RAW MATERIAL   SHARED     collect broadly as Ya Selva; both projects draw from one well
RELEASES       SEPARATE   "I don't really wanna share releases"
```

**Raw material: shared.** Adam: *"if I was collecting them as Ya Selva, it wouldn't be
an issue."* Right — **collection context is what settles it.**

**Releases: separate.** This is the correct line. Sharing raw material is fine. Sharing
releases is where it gets muddy — and not for ethical reasons, but because **he already
has too many names, and blurring these two is how the palette discipline dies.** A track
that's really a Ya Selva song wearing a BTL credit (or the reverse) is the failure mode
to avoid.

## The asymmetry — why direction matters

The two directions are not equivalent:

| direction | status |
|---|---|
| **Ya Selva collects → feeds BTL** | ✅ No tension whatsoever. Same act, two outputs. Every Ya Selva trip grows the thing that's currently stalled. **This is the default.** |
| **BTL corpus → Ya Selva release** | ⚠ Needs care. Material contributed *to* BTL, under BTL's framing, appearing on a solo release is the case that can read wrong. |

The BTL corpus is the platform's actual asset — contributions from artists in 20+
countries, gathered on a specific promise about how they'd be used. Not a legal question
so much as a *"what did people think they were signing up for"* question.

Pulling from it works cleanly **if** it's framed as the platform's founder demonstrating
the thing publicly, **and** contributors get credited on Ya Selva releases exactly as
they would on a BTL one. It becomes uncomfortable if it reads as the guy with the keys
quietly using the collective's material for his solo record.

**The collection-consent rule makes this moot going forward.** It only leaves the
existing corpus as a legacy case, since that material was gathered before the rule
existed.

## Why this reinforces rather than complicates

Same principle as `09-the-shared-dna.md`, applied operationally. If the shared DNA is
**people making music together**, then telling people up front where their sound might
travel *is* the participatory posture. Asking afterward is the extractive one.

It also strengthens the Ya Selva practice: he's out there recording anyway — go out,
meet people, listen, make something public. The rule costs nothing and converts every
trip into material for both projects.

Sits alongside rule 6 in `01-the-connection.md`: **credit, consent, compensation from
day one.** This is what that looks like at the moment of recording.
