# Modern One-of-One Sound — the identity problem

Creative direction. 2026-09-01. **Very important identity note.**

```
THE GOAL IS NOT TO MAKE "GOOD WORLD-ELECTRONIC MUSIC."
THE GOAL IS TO MAKE YA SELVA IMPOSSIBLE TO CONFUSE WITH ANYONE ELSE.
```

## The breakthrough

Adam imagined Ya Selva mixed with **Radiohead** and **Tool** — through rhythm,
heaviness, and modern production rather than surface-level genre imitation.

### Radiohead / "Idioteque"

What stands out is the kick. The sound itself is excellent, but more importantly the
**pattern is more intricate than a conventional beat would be**.

Which means some of the "tribal" or world-rhythm parts Adam imagines don't all have
to be literal hand drums. Some of those rhythmic roles can be electronic.

> Electronic percussion can participate in the rhythm as an intelligent voice rather
> than simply marking a four-on-the-floor pulse.

### Tool

Tool is "tribal" in a completely different way: modern, industrial, heavy,
polyrhythmic. Tool proves that **polyrhythm can feel enormous, dark, physical and
aggressive** rather than delicate or ethnographic.

That opens a bridge. Ya Selva can stay rooted in world rhythmic logic while letting
distorted electric guitar, odd-meter and polyrhythmic structures, and heavier
dynamics into the language.

Adam's thought: *what if world music met Tool?* Not as a gimmick or a fusion
exercise — because both traditions already share a deep concern with cyclical
rhythm, interlocking parts, trance, repetition and bodily intensity.

## The anti-cosplay problem

Adam explicitly does **not** want Ya Selva to become indigenous cosplay or a
collection of obvious "world music" signifiers.

A possible solution:

```
TRADITIONAL / GLOBAL RHYTHMIC IDEAS ARE SOURCE CODE, NOT THE FINISHED AESTHETIC.
```

Use the deeper principles — cyclical time, polyrhythm, interlocking rhythm,
call-and-response, drones, pentatonic/modal language, communal singing, repetition,
physical groove. But let the surface instrumentation come from Adam's actual
contemporary life:

| the palette | |
|---|---|
| acoustic guitar | electric guitar |
| laptop / sampler | electronic kicks |
| electronic percussion | voice |
| found sound | one or two hand-played instruments |
| future flute / winds / horns | |

The result should not announce "WORLD MUSIC." It should sound like **one specific
person has metabolized many musical worlds into a new language.**

## The core modernizing principle

```
ANCIENT RHYTHMIC LOGIC, CONTEMPORARY MATERIALS.
```

Or more directly:

> What would these musical impulses sound like if they passed through Radiohead,
> Tool, an MPC, a broken speaker, electric guitar, and Adam's own body in 2026?

The goal is not trendy production. The goal is to **stop treating organic and
traditional material as the finished aesthetic** and start treating it as raw musical
intelligence that can inhabit completely modern sound design.

## Bowed electric guitar — the live-show image

Adam also imagined **bowed electric guitar**, à la Sigur Rós, as a dramatic
live-performance device. With the right electric guitar, a bow could create an
enormous harmonic wash before the drums arrive.

A possible live transition:

```
1  everything drops out
2  bowed electric guitar — a huge ugly/beautiful drone or harmonic cloud
3  one low drum enters
4  an asymmetrical electronic kick pattern starts appearing underneath
5  the polyrhythm assembles piece by piece
6  full percussion / bass / distorted guitar / voice DETONATES
7  guitar down — Adam physically dances and leads the room
```

Step 7 is the point of the whole sequence: it gets him **out from behind an
instrument**. And the whole move creates band-sized dynamic range without requiring a
band, and without relying completely on backing tracks.

## The deeper identity question

The governing challenge now:

> **How do I make Ya Selva one of one — so no one else in the world sounds like this
> project?**

The answer may come from the collision itself:

```
world rhythmic intelligence
+ modern electronic percussion
+ art-rock harmony / sound design
+ genuinely heavy guitar
+ participatory live ritual
+ bard / traveler songwriting
```

**The important move is not copying Radiohead or Tool. It is borrowing their
permission structures.**

| source | the permission it grants |
|---|---|
| **Radiohead** | a groove can be electronic, anxious, awkward, asymmetrical, beautiful — and still deeply emotional |
| **Tool** | repetition can become ritual; polymeter can be physical; heaviness can be hypnotic rather than macho |
| **West African / Afro-diasporic / global** | rhythm can be a network of interacting voices instead of "drums accompanying the song" |
| **Björk-type thinking** | each song can create its own sonic ecology rather than staying inside one fixed production template |

## Candidate fingerprint rules

- Never default to generic organic-house rhythm.
- If hand percussion is present, pair it with at least one sound that clearly belongs
  to the present or future.
- Let kicks and bass **participate** in polyrhythm instead of merely anchoring it.
- Use distortion and heaviness as dramatic color, not as a separate "rock section."
- Let acoustic beauty sit directly beside brutal electronic or electric sound.
- Keep modal / open harmonic space when useful, but allow stranger jazz-informed
  harmonic intelligence to enter.
- Every arrangement should contain **at least one moment that could only happen in a
  live Ya Selva performance.**

## Search method

The final sound probably will not be discovered intellectually. Build **mutant
sketches** that collide musical worlds, and listen for repeated moments where the
result suddenly feels unmistakably Ya Selva.

```
Poranguí                  ×  Idioteque
Ali Farka Touré           ×  Tool
Bobby McFerrin            ×  industrial percussion
cumbia rhythmic logic     ×  bowed electric-guitar drones
West African interlock    ×  distorted sub kick
acoustic bard song        ×  enormous polyrhythmic ending
```

Most will fail. **The goal is to hear the same identity emerge three or four times.**
Once that happens, the project has moved from searching for a genre to discovering
its own grammar.

## The governing line

> Ya Selva shouldn't sound "global." It should sound like one specific person
> traveled through many musical worlds and metabolized them into a new language.
