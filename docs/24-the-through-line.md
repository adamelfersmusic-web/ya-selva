# The Through-Line — One Vision Since 17

> **KEYSTONE CAPTURE · 2026-08-07.** Adam traced the entire lineage of his creative vision unprompted and arrived at the synthesis in real time. This note exists because the lineage explains the stall better than any project-level analysis has.

---

## 1 · THE LINEAGE

| age | form the vision took |
|---|---|
| ~17 | participatory art. An ensemble — music and performance art hybrid |
| ~22–30 | a public performance project: shows up unannounced in town squares and streets. Multiple vocalists, horns, percussion. Bizarre visual props. A flag or giant sign so people go *"what the fuck was that"* and look it up |
| ~25 | **"The Universal Hum"** — sampling insects and sounds from around the world. The sound of humanity. *Baraka* meets *1 Giant Leap* |
| — | finds *1 Giant Leap*: **"that's pretty much what I want to do in my life."** Then Playing for Change |
| 20 yrs | **200+ facilitated events. 100+ workshops.** Drum circles, song circles, ecstatic vocalization, improvised gatherings |
| — | Break This Loop v1: a monthly curated **loop pack**. ~4 hours to make one. "Really good ingredients, you can't cook a bad meal." Sent to a friend, who had fun with it |
| — | Break This Loop v2 (current): global, online, one song at a time, infinitely remixable, all mediums. *"Burning Man, but online"* |
| now | **the synthesis** — tour flywheel |

**It has always been the same idea.** Participatory art, made by many people, in a form that makes strangers into participants. Every phase is one vision wearing different equipment.

## 2 · THE DEMONSTRATED GIFT — and it is not the one the current project uses

**What 20 years of evidence says he is exceptional at:**

> **Facilitating participatory music in physical rooms.**

- 200+ drum circles, song circles, gatherings · 100+ workshops
- It worked *everywhere he did it* — and it is what generated his festival performance opportunities across the country
- **The proof case:** his car broke down, he had no percussion instruments, so he ran it with only his voice — improvised call-and-response — and it went off harder than the instrument version. That led to the spontaneous vocal choir work he then toured through the US and Latin America.

A gift that survives losing all its equipment is not a technique. It is the thing itself.

## 3 · ⭐ THE DIAGNOSIS

**Break This Loop v2's online-first form is the one version of his lifelong vision that does not run on his actual gift.**

| BTL v2 requires | Adam's demonstrated strength |
|---|---|
| asynchronous, remote | **in-person, in a room** |
| infrastructure, database, pipeline | facilitation, presence, voice |
| editing capacity at volume | improvisation and read-the-room |
| a team he had to assemble | himself, alone, with nothing |

The stall was never discipline or vision. **He built the version that runs on infrastructure instead of the version that runs on him.**

## 4 · THE CAUSAL DETAIL THAT EXPLAINS THE SCOPE-UP

BTL v1 — the loop pack — was small, shippable, and worked. Then:

```
meniscus tear → could not walk for ~3 months
              → started using AI in that window
              → the vision went global, online, infinite
```

**The vision migrated online during the exact period he physically could not be in a room with people.** That is not a coincidence and it is not a failure of judgment — it is what the vision had to become when the room was unavailable.

**He is not immobile now.** The room is available again. That, structurally, is what the tour flywheel restores.

## 5 · THE PARALLEL PROJECT HE KEPT SEPARATE

His live set: **ritual organic electronica — participatory, tribal, fun.** His own stated coordinates: Peggy Gou × Jacob Collier's community choir × Bonobo × Moshe/MOSC. Community singing and movement fused with his production, rather than a drum circle or a DJ set.

He has held this and Break This Loop as **two projects.** 2026-08-07 is the first time he considered that they might be one.

> The live set is BTL v2's missing engine. BTL is the live set's missing distribution.

## 6 · THE ROUNDS STRUCTURE HE PROPOSED

```
TOUR → collect footage for ~4 songs simultaneously across many events
     → over ~a year
     → release ONE at a time; each release LAUNCHES that song's remix ritual
     → a mini-tour during each launch collects more footage
       AND gives people a live campaign to join
```

**Why this works, in his own words:** *"when the campaign's live, people are motivated to be involved."* That is the real engine. The tour is not the point — the tour is what makes a campaign **live in a place**. Urgency plus proximity is what converts an audience into participants.

**This is structurally Whitacre's rounds model** (VC1→VC6, each larger) with one difference nobody in the reference class had: **the rounds are also live events.**

## ⭐ THE LINEAGE CLOSES — 2026-08-07, later the same session

After working through the whole naming/structure problem, Adam landed here:

> *"A band is what I've always wanted to do. That's my fucking passion. If this grew into a band that somehow bridged both projects, it would be the coolest life I could ever imagine."*

**Look at row one of the lineage table above.** Age ~17: *an ensemble — music and performance art together, a hybrid.*

```
AGE 17:  an ensemble
NOW:     a band that bridges both projects
```

**Twenty-five years, and the answer is the same word.** Every project in between — the town-square performance group, the Universal Hum, Break This Loop v1 and v2, Ya Selva — has been a version of the ensemble *without the ensemble.* The band is not a year-three add-on bolted onto the strategy. It may be the actual destination, and everything else is either scaffolding toward it or the means of funding and finding it.

### ⚠ But bands cannot be founded on schedule

They accrete around someone who is already visibly doing the thing. Playing for Change's touring band formed after years of Songs Around the World. Medicine for the People formed around Nahko already playing.

**Which reframes the Village nights and documentary tours entirely: they are the audition process.** Every producer who runs a breakout room, every instrumentalist who shows up, every collaborator on a documentary tour is a candidate. He is not waiting until year three to start the band — **he starts recruiting the moment he runs night one.**
